## P2 direct durable replay evidence

`startOpenSeaMirror` no longer creates a second full `snapshot.ndjson` copy.
The persistent OpenSea spool is replayed directly to the snapshot sink through
a capacity-one asynchronous handoff. The slow-consumer regression proves that
the producer cannot advance beyond one unpublished event, preserving bounded
one-in/one-out backpressure.

Backfill supports a deferred-finalize lease with idempotent `commit` and
`abort` operations. The source spool, cursor checkpoints, and ownership lock
remain authoritative until the snapshot sink commits. A sink upload failure
aborts the lease, preserves the source payload and checkpoints, and releases
the active lock; a subsequent retry consumes the preserved source, succeeds,
and removes the known payload while still holding exclusion.

The focused suite also proves:

- zero-history and precommit-only cutovers, including an all-zero cutover;
- no second snapshot-sized file while replay is in progress;
- canonical precommit-to-postcommit ordering;
- stale-lock and successor-writer safety;

- post-commit events drain only after the initial snapshot commit.
